Industry Context

Global Capability Centers (GCCs), also called Captives, are offshore arms of multinational companies — typically in India — running technology R&D, finance, risk, operations, data, cybersecurity, and other specialist functions for the parent. India hosts more than 1,700 active GCCs employing 1.6M+ professionals, with new entrants from the US, UK, and Europe setting up greenfield centres every quarter. Hiring at a GCC is a different game from hiring for an India-headquartered company: greenfield setups need to ramp from 0 to 500+ headcount in 12–18 months, lateral attrition runs 22–28% in the first 18 months, and every candidate has to clear the parent company’s hiring bar — not just the local one.

Key hiring challenges in GCC / Captive units:

  • Greenfield ramp pressure — scaling from zero to functional centre within parent-committed timelines
  • Niche specialist roles (FP&A with US GAAP/IFRS, treasury, GRC, cybersecurity, data engineering, AI/ML, FinOps) competing for the same talent pool as product companies and big tech
  • Long parent-company offer approval cycles (2–3 weeks) during which candidates accept competing offers
  • Global communication readiness and parent-stakeholder fluency are non-negotiable — generic recruiters miss this signal
  • High lateral attrition — competing GCCs poach within 18–24 months of joining
  • Need for India market knowledge (statutory compliance, labour law, tax) for hires interfacing with regulators
